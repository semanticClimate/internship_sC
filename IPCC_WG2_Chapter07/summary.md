# Summary of IPCC AR6 WG2 Chapter 7 : Health, Wellbeing and the Changing Structure of Communities
 Climate hazards are increasingly driving voluntary migration and displacement and are also a contributing factor to violent conflict (high confidence).
Climate change negatively affects human health by increasing the risk of illnesses, premature deaths, malnutrition, and mental health challenges. Extreme events are often followed by higher rates of mental illness in affected populations (very high confidence).
Heat-related health risks are rising due to urbanization, more frequent extreme temperatures, and aging populations. By 2050, climate change is projected to cause an excess of 250,000 deaths annually, largely from heat stress, malaria, and diarrheal diseases, with over half of these deaths expected to occur in Africa (compared to 1961 levels).
Children, adolescents (especially girls), elderly individuals, and people with existing physical or mental health conditions are particularly vulnerable.
However, future impacts on vector-borne diseases can be significantly reduced through integrated vector control, disease surveillance, early warning systems, and vaccine development.
Adaptation and sustainable development are key to reducing conflict and building peace in climate-vulnerable regions. Environmental peacebuilding—through natural resource sharing and climate-resilient practices—can address the root causes of grievances and reduce vulnerability to climate change.
Importantly, the net global financial benefits of health-related co-benefits, such as fewer hospitalizations and deaths, are expected to exceed the costs of climate change mitigation (high confidence).
